# Domain justification — network targets for an Engineering Management graduate on 12-month OPT

## Executive summary

This page explains who the recipe is for, what they cannot see without it, and where it fits in a job-search day. In short: an engineering-management graduate with about three months left on a twelve-month work permit cannot easily tell which companies have actually sponsored visas for project- or production-management work, which of those have nothing open right now, or which sponsorship records are attached to the wrong company. The recipe makes that visible from checked records, turns the "nothing open" companies into a networking list, and stops at the visa questions only a person can answer.

## Who, in exactly what situation

An international student who finished an MS in Engineering Management, is on 12-month post-completion F-1 OPT with no STEM extension claimed, and whose OPT ends in December 2026 — about 91 days after the run date. They target project-management (SOC 13-1082) and industrial-production-management (SOC 11-3051) roles, so they need an employer that sponsors H-1B petitions for *that* kind of work, not just any work. Generic sponsor lists, dominated by software roles, describe someone else.

## The information asymmetry

From outside, this person cannot easily see:

1. **Who has sponsored a role like theirs.** Sponsor lists rank by volume; a company with thousands of software approvals looks like a safe bet for a project manager, and may not be.
2. **Who sponsors but has nothing open.** A sponsor with no matching opening is invisible to a job-board search, yet it is the best informational-interview target: the conversation happens before the role is posted.
3. **Whose record is real.** In the shipped data, 42 groups covering 85 companies share an identical sponsorship record under different names (e.g. CONVEY INC / COVEY INC), and every one of the 1,557 approval and denial counts is even, which genuine counts would not be.

## Engine layers

- **80 Days to Stay** — sponsorship history and funding columns (the H-1B record, label *record*).
- **Job-Ops** — board liveness through the repository's scanner; liveness is a gate, and a missing check holds the company instead of counting as open.
- **The Cognitive Pivot** — SOC titles from the BLS/O*NET compact file as the matching vocabulary (no wages, no ability levels; role quality has zero weight in the scorer and is not used).

## Where it fits the 3-3-2 day

It takes over the **research half of the two research-and-apply hours**: finding which companies sponsor this kind of role and whether each has a matching opening. It leaves the tailoring half to the person, and only after the visa-path gate. Its main output feeds the **three networking hours**: the "network first" list is a list of people to ask for an informational conversation. Building and honestly testing it is itself credibility-hour work.

**Time saved — an estimate, not a measurement.** Assumptions: about 12–18 minutes per company to check sponsorship history, funding, and the careers page by hand; a working list of about 20 companies a week. That is 4–6 hours a week by hand. With this recipe the record steps take seconds; what remains is roughly 3–5 minutes per board that has no public API, plus the person's gate checks — about 1–1.5 hours. Estimated saving: **about 3–4 hours a week**. Not timed against a real week.

## Domain failure modes, and who would struggle to catch them

1. **A borrowed sponsorship record.** A student with weeks left sees "38 approvals, 100% approved" on a clean-looking row and writes to the company, but an upstream fuzzy join attached a lookalike company's record. Hardest for exactly the person under time pressure, and an AI summary repeats the row fluently. The recipe flags shared records and makes identity a human gate.
2. **A title that isn't the occupation.** "Technical Program Manager" reads like project management to an engineering-management graduate; the filing may have been a software role under a computer-occupation code, with different requirements. Hardest for non-software applicants, for whom this distinction decides whether the record is relevant at all. The recipe labels the match as an inference and caps ambiguous titles at a lower tier.
3. **"Apply" read as "safe".** For a 12-month OPT ending in December, an offer from a cap-subject employer does not by itself bridge the gap to the next H-1B registration. The recipe never says it does; tailoring waits for the person's DSO or attorney sign-off.
